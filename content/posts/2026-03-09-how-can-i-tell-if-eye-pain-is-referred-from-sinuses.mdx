Eye pain that actually originates from your sinuses is a classic example of "referred pain." This occurs because the nerve branches from both your eyes and your sinuses converge along the trigeminal nerve pathway. Because these signals travel the same route, the brain sometimes misinterprets sinus pressure as pain coming from the eye itself. You can identify if your eye pain is likely referred from your sinuses by looking for a few specific indicators:
Changes with movement: The most distinct sign of sinus-related eye pain is that the headache or facial soreness worsens or changes when you adjust your head position. Location of the pain: The discomfort is typically felt deep behind the eyes, rather than on the surface of the eye. Appearance of the eye: Unlike many eye-specific issues (like infections or abrasions), the eye area itself will usually look completely normal, though some individuals might experience a bit of swelling around the outside of the eye. While sinus pressure is a common cause of referred pain, you should consult a doctor if the pain is severe, does not improve, progressively worsens, or is accompanied by other symptoms like vision changes, noticeable redness, or nausea
