Imagine a subtle intruder entering your home every night, removing one tiny pixel from the edges of your living room. For months, you notice nothing—your central view remains perfectly sharp. By the time you realize you are looking at the world through a narrow cardboard tube, the edges of your vision are gone forever.

This is the exact mechanism of glaucoma—often termed "the silent thief of sight." It is a progressive eye condition that damages the optic nerve so quietly that most patients lose significant vision before experiencing a single symptom.

## The Hydraulics of the Human Eye

Think of the eye as a delicate fluid-filled sphere. The ciliary body continuously produces a clear fluid called aqueous humor to nourish internal structures. This fluid normally drains out through a complex sponge-like meshwork.

When this drainage canal becomes clogged or inefficient, fluid builds up, raising intraocular pressure (IOP). Elevated pressure places mechanical stress directly onto the optic nerve at the back of the eye. Over time, this pressure starves delicate nerve fibers of oxygen, causing them to wither away permanently.

## Why Is Glaucoma So Deceptive?

The most common form—open-angle glaucoma—causes zero pain, zero redness, and zero visual cloudiness in its early stages. Because the brain automatically paints over blind spots using input from the other eye, peripheral vision erodes unnoticed.

Key risk factors include:

- Genetics (a 4-9x higher risk if immediate family members have glaucoma)
- Age over 40-50
- High myopia or hyperopia
- Thin corneal central thickness

Fortunately, modern 3D Optical Coherence Tomography (OCT) allows eye specialists to scan the thickness of the optic nerve head at a microscopic level, detecting early changes years before visual field loss occurs.
