For anyone who has worn glasses or contact lenses for years, the dream of waking up and seeing the world clearly is powerful. But when you start thinking about laser eye surgery, a lot of questions pop up: Is it safe? How do they know my eyes are ready? Will it hurt?

Many people believe that finding out if you are suitable for laser surgery is just about reading letters off a standard wall chart. But in reality, reading letters on a chart only tells an eye specialist about 25% of what they need to know. It is like trying to buy a house based only on a photo of the front door.

To ensure your safety and give you clear vision, a truly comprehensive suitabilty exam—like the KSA Eye Audit—dives deep beneath the surface of your eyes. This thorough testing is what makes KSA's touch-free, blade-free Flow3 laser procedure so precise, safe, and successful.

Let's take a warm, professional, and easy-to-understand look at what actually happens during a next-generation eye exam, and how it paves your way to visual freedom.

## Understanding Your Eye's Unique Landscape

During a standard eye exam, you sit in a chair, look at a wall, and read: "E, T, O, L, P...". While this measures your central visual acuity, it does not show the unique physical landscape of your eye.

When you go for a comprehensive suitability assessment (known as a sobivusuuring or Nägemise Audit in KSA), optometrists like Helena Nisu look at several crucial factors:

### Corneal Topography and Thickness (The Eye's Map)

The cornea is the clear window at the front of your eye. It acts like the lens of a camera, bending light so you can see. But just like no two fingerprints are the same, no two corneas are shaped exactly alike.

Using advanced mapping technology, specialists create a highly detailed, 3D topographical map of your cornea. This scan reveals every microscopic "hill and valley" on your eye's surface. Measuring the exact thickness of the cornea is also vital because laser surgery gently reshapes this clear window, and we must ensure your eye has plenty of healthy tissue to support safe, stable healing.

### Dry Eye Diagnostics (Checking the Soil)

For a seed to grow, the soil must be moist. Similarly, for an eye to heal beautifully after a laser procedure, your tear film must be healthy.

KSA's audit includes specialized diagnostics to measure the quantity and quality of your tears. If your eyes are too dry, the clinic will help you restore your tear film health before any procedure is scheduled to ensure a smooth, comfortable recovery.

### Vision Stability and Eye Coordination (The Teamwork Check)

A stable prescription is a prerequisite for laser surgery. Specialists verify your vision history to make sure your eyes have stopped changing. They also check how your eyes work together as a team (eye coordination). This ensures that once your nearsightedness is corrected, your eyes will focus together in harmony.

## The Path to Freedom: Why KSA Chooses Flow3

Once your detailed diagnostic audit confirms that your eyes are healthy and suitable, the door to visual freedom opens.

Historically, older generations of laser surgery (like traditional LASIK) required cutting a thin slice, or "flap," in the cornea. While effective, the idea of cutting can feel frightening, and a corneal flap requires careful, long-term care.

This is why KSA Silmakeskus specializes in Flow3—a modern, touch-free and blade-free laser procedure.

**No Cutting, No Blading.** The Flow3 laser works directly on the outer surface of your cornea. The laser system reshapes the microscopic curve of your eye in just a couple of minutes without ever physically touching your eye.

**Safe and Comfortable.** Because there is no cut and no flap, the structural strength of your eye is completely preserved. The recovery is rapid, and you are supported by a dedicated 24/7 care team.

**The Joy of Clear Stars.** For many patients, the results make a real difference. KSA patients often share that their favorite moments of freedom are simple, everyday wonders—like looking up at the night sky and seeing individual stars clearly for the first time, practicing sports without foggy glasses, or waking up in the morning and immediately seeing the clock across the room.
