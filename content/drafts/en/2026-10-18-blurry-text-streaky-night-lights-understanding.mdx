Have you ever noticed bright streetlights turning into long, glowing streaks when you drive at night? Or found yourself squinting at your phone screen because the letters look slightly stretched or doubled?

If so, you are likely experiencing astigmatism. Astigmatism is not an eye disease—it is simply a very common focusing condition. In fact, almost everyone has a tiny bit of astigmatism, but when it starts causing blurry vision or tired eyes, it is time to take a closer look.

What Is Astigmatism? (The Football vs. Soccer Ball Analogy)
To understand astigmatism, think about the shape of two sports balls. A perfectly shaped eye is round like a soccer ball. Light passes through it evenly and focuses on a single, sharp point on the retina at the back of the eye.

With astigmatism, the front surface of the eye (the cornea) is curved more like an oval football or rugby ball. Because the curve is steeper in one direction than another, light bends unevenly as it enters the eye. Instead of one crisp focus point, light scatters across multiple points, making objects near and far look blurry or stretched out.

Common Symptoms You Might Recognize:
Night Driving Struggles: Headlights and streetlamps stretch into long lines, glare, or bright halos.
Stretched or Shadows on Text: Reading small print causes eye fatigue or double-vision effects.
Frequent Headaches: Squinting and trying to force your eyes to focus leads to forehead tension.
Glasses Never Feel Quite Right: Objects feel almost clear with glasses, but never truly razor-sharp.
How Is Astigmatism Corrected?
Glasses with "cylinder" lenses or special toric contact lenses can bend light to compensate for the oval shape. However, contact lenses can dry out your eyes during long workdays, and glasses can restrict an active lifestyle.
